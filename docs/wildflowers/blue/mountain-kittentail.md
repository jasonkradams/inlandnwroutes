# Mountain Kittentail (Synthyris missurica)

Mountain Kittentail (*Synthyris missurica*) is a perennial wildflower found in scattered areas of Washington,
Oregon, California, and Idaho. It is easy to overlook until its erect spike of deep blue flowers appears,
sometimes among the very first blooms of spring.

---

## Overview & Botanical Profile

The species forms a low clump of nearly round, hairless basal leaves, each 1 to 3 inches across, cut into
rounded lobes or teeth and carried on long petioles. From this clump rises a single erect, hairy flower
stem bearing a 2 to 7 inch spike of many small, mid to deep blue flowers. Each flower's upper lobe is
slightly larger than the other three, and bluish stamens project beyond the petals.

Mountain Kittentail grows in moist to dry shade, typically among rocks or in openings within forests, at mid
to high elevations. A similar subspecies, *stellata*, has larger flowers and brown hairs on its short flower
stalks.

## A Chance Encounter

On a hike with my co-author David, from Frosty Baldy to Latour Baldy, I stumbled upon this wildflower that I
had never seen before. When I got home, I searched the web to see what I had found, and it turned out to be
a Mountain Kittentail. Although the species isn't rare, I haven't been able to fill in every detail above —
I'll fill in the blanks as I find them.
