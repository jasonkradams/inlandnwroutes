## Smartphone & iPad Photography Tips

Modern smartphones and tablets pack sophisticated sensors capable of producing incredible landscape images when properly adjusted.

### Focus & Exposure Box Control

Digital phone sensors are programmed to produce bright images, which often leads to washed-out, overexposed skies.

1. **Set the Focus Point:** Tap your phone screen over your primary subject. A yellow focus square will appear. Whatever is inside that square becomes your sharp focus point.
2. **Adjust Exposure (The Sun / Asterisk Slider):** Next to the yellow focus square, notice the small sun or asterisk icon. Touch and drag that icon **downward** to darken the exposure. Darkening the exposure brings out deep blue skies, rich cloud contrast, and natural foliage colors.
3. **Low-Light Adjustments:** In dark shadows or dusk conditions, drag the icon **upward** slightly to boost shadow detail.

### Panorama & Video Sweep Technique

- **Start High:** When capturing panoramas or video sweeps, start your composition near the top of the frame.
- **Set Exposure:** Tap the sky area and drag the exposure slider down until the sky tones look rich and saturated.
- **Sweep Upward:** Start your pan or video sweep moving slightly upward or across rather than downward to prevent the sky from overexposing as you move.

### Power & Care

- **Cold Weather Battery Drain:** Cold mountain temperatures drain phone batteries quickly. Carry a portable power bank and charging cables.
- **Clean Lenses:** Keep phone camera lenses free of fingerprints, trail dust, and moisture.

## Digital Camera Fundamentals (DSLR & Mirrorless)

### Dust Prevention

Dust and moisture are a sensor's worst enemies. **Never change lenses in dusty, windy, or dirty environments.** Keep your camera bag spotless inside.

### Media Card Management & Reformatting

- **Reformat in Camera:** Download your daily photos to your computer or hard drive first. Reinsert the card into your camera and use the camera menu to **Reformat** the card. Reformatting clears the file index cleanly and restores full capacity.
- **Never Delete Photos In-Camera:** Deleting individual photos on your card creates fragmented storage voids. If you insert a new photo of a different file size, the card can become corrupted.
- **Corrupted Card Protocol:** If a card becomes corrupted or unreadable, **do not take more photos on it**. Take the card to a professional camera shop (such as [Camera Corral](camera-corral.md)) to recover your image files.

## Exposure Triangle & Light Meters

Camera light meters balance three fundamental elements. Adjusting one element directly impacts the other two:

| Element | Definition & Function | Field Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ISO / ASA** | Sensor sensitivity to light | Use **ISO 100** for bright daylight. Increase to **ISO 400+** for low light or fast action *(higher ISO increases image noise/grain)*. |
| **Shutter Speed** | Duration the sensor is exposed to light | Use **1/500s or faster** to freeze action (wildlife, running water). Use **1/2s or slower** (on a tripod) for silky waterfall effects. |
| **Aperture (f-stop)** | Volume of light passing through the lens | Use **f/3.5 – f/5.6** for shallow depth of field (blurs background). Use **f/11 – f/22** for deep front-to-back landscape sharpness. |

## DSLR & Mirrorless Shooting Modes

Avoid using the fully automatic "green camera" mode, which deprives you of creative control. Use these dedicated exposure modes instead:

- **Program Mode (`P`):** Auto-exposure convenience with the ability to shift shutter speeds or apertures using the command dial.
- **Shutter Priority (`S` / `Tv`):** You select the shutter speed (e.g., 1/1000s for flying birds), and the camera selects the matching aperture.
- **Aperture Priority (`A` / `Av`):** You select the aperture (e.g., f/11 for landscapes), and the camera selects the matching shutter speed. *Check your shutter speed to prevent camera shake if it drops below 1/100s!*
- **Manual Mode (`M`):** Full manual control over both shutter speed and aperture for tricky lighting or tripod night photography.
- **Exposure Compensation:** Set exposure compensation to **-0.3 EV to -1.3 EV** in bright sunlight to protect sky highlights from blowing out.

## Essential Camera Accessories & Lenses

### Tripods & Cable Releases

- **Tripod:** The single most valuable photography accessory. Required for slow-shutter waterfall photography, dusk landscapes, and night star photos.
- **Cable / Remote Release:** Replaces pressing the shutter button by hand, eliminating camera vibration on long exposures. Alternatively, set your camera's built-in 2-second shutter timer.

### Lens Selection & Care

- **Landscape Trio:** A versatile kit includes a Super-Wide-Angle lens (16–35mm), Medium Telephoto (24–70mm), and Telephoto Zoom (70–200mm).
- **Pro-Grade Glass:** High-end glass (*Nikon ED* or *Canon L-Series*) provides superior edge-to-edge sharpness and color rendition over cheap kit lenses.
- **Zooming Vacuum Warning:** Avoid zooming rapidly in dusty environments. Zooming creates a temporary vacuum inside the lens barrel that can suck dust particles onto internal elements.

### Landscape Lens Filters

| Filter Type | Primary Purpose | Recommended Usage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Circular Polarizer (CPL)** | Cuts reflections from water/foliage; deepens blue skies | Essential for mountain landscapes and streams (B+W CPL recommended). |
| **Graduated Neutral Density (GND)** | Darkens bright skies while keeping dark foregrounds exposed | Ideal for high-contrast sunrise and sunset mountain horizons. |
| **Warming Filter** | Tones down cool, bluish mountain light with warm tones | Adds rich warmth to overcast or shaded scenes. |
| **UV / Skylight Filter** | Protects the front glass element from scratches, dust, and water spray | Cheap insurance to protect expensive lens glass. |

## Critical Sensor Cleaning Warning

!!! danger "Do NOT Clean Sensors Yourself"

    Camera sensors are covered by extremely thin, fragile glass filters. Attempting to clean the sensor yourself can scratch the glass or trigger the mechanical shutter to snap shut on your cleaning tool, causing catastrophic camera damage. Always have your sensor cleaned by professionals at a trusted shop like [Camera Corral](camera-corral.md).
