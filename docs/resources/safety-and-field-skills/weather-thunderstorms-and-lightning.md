## Pre-Trip Weather & Smoke Planning Tools

Checking multi-source weather and air quality tools before heading into the mountains significantly reduces risks from severe storms, flash floods, or hazardous wildfire smoke.

### Core Forecasting Resources

1. **NOAA & Weather.gov:** [Weather.gov](https://www.weather.gov) is the gold standard for mountain forecasting. Read the daily text discussion, then inspect the **"Hourly Weather Forecast"** graph (located down the right panel) for hourly temperature, wind speed, precipitation potential, and thunderstorm odds.
2. **EPA AirNow:** [AirNow.gov](https://www.airnow.gov) provides real-time Particulate Matter (PM2.5) air quality readings to identify where wildfire smoke threatens health.
3. **Windy.com:** [Windy.com](https://www.windy.com) allows you to overlay real-time wind speed, wind direction, and PM2.5 smoke plumes to project where smoke will blow.
4. **InciWeb:** [InciWeb](https://inciweb.nwcg.gov) details active wildfire perimeters, containment percentages, and management incident reports.
5. **NOAA GeoColor Satellite:** Use NOAA's daytime *GeoColor* and *GLM FED3+GeoColor* satellite loops to track real-time cloud development, thunderstorm initiation, and smoke plume trajectories.

## The Science of Thunderstorms & Precipitation

At any given moment, roughly **2,000 thunderstorms** are active across the Earth, producing about **100 lightning strikes per second** (over 8 million strikes per day).

### Cloud Dynamics & Water Weight

- **Updraft & Downdraft Mechanics:** Solar radiation heats the Earth's surface, causing warm, moisture-laden air to rise rapidly. As it rises, it cools and condenses into towering cumulonimbus clouds. Cold air sinks, creating powerful downdrafts and turbulent updrafts.
- **Massive Water Content:** A large thundercloud can hold **150,000 tons of water**—enough to fill a pond 300 feet wide, one mile long, and 5 feet deep.
- **Rainfall Volume:** A heavy 2-inch rainfall deposits **90 lbs of water per square yard** (over 200 tons of water per acre). Across the United States, over 4 trillion gallons of precipitation fall daily.

### Worldwide Regional Storm Frequencies

- **Rocky Mountains:** Experience **50 to 70 thunderstorm days** annually.
- **Kampala, Uganda:** One of the stormiest places on Earth, averaging **242 thunderstorm days** per year.
- **Florida:** Averages up to **46 lightning strikes per square mile** each year.
- **Polar Regions:** Cold, stable air in the Arctic and Antarctic limits thunderstorms to roughly once every 10 years.

## Lightning Physics, Thunder & Ecological Benefits

### Temperature & Distance Calculation

- **Extreme Heat:** The air inside a lightning channel heats to **27,000°F** in microseconds—nearly three times hotter than the surface of the Sun (10,000°F).
- **Thunder Mechanics:** Superheated air expands explosively, creating acoustic pressure shockwaves audible up to 7 miles away.
- **The 5-Second Distance Rule:** Count the seconds between seeing a lightning flash and hearing the thunderclap. **Every 5 seconds equals 1 mile of distance.** (If thunder sounds within 30 seconds of a flash, lightning is within 6 miles).

### Ecological & Forest Dynamics

- **Natural Nitrogen Fertilizer:** Earth's atmosphere is 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen. High-voltage lightning strikes chemically combine nitrogen and oxygen into nitrates. Rain washes over **100 million tons of natural nitrogen fertilizer** into global soil each year—far exceeding human industrial output.
- **Wildfire History:** Lightning triggers half of all forest wildfires. During a 10-day dry lightning event in June 1940, **1,488 fires** were ignited across the Northern Rockies.
- **Tree Strike Physics:** If a tree's bark is wet from rain, electricity travels safely down the external film of water to the ground. If the bark is dry, current travels through internal sap channels; the sap instantly vaporizes and explodes the tree apart. Rough-barked oaks suffer the greatest damage.

## Backcountry Lightning Safety Protocols

!!! warning "The 30-30 Rule"

    If the time between seeing lightning and hearing thunder is **30 seconds or less**, seek immediate shelter. Remain in shelter for **30 minutes** after hearing the last clap of thunder.

### Vehicle & Indoor Safety

- **Vehicles as Faraday Shields:** Enclosed metal vehicles (not convertibles or open ATVs) offer excellent protection. Rubber tires do not insulate; rather, the outer metal frame directs current around the occupants into the ground. Avoid touching internal metal components.
- **Indoor Precautions:** Stay away from plumbing, corded phones, electrical outlets, windows, and doors. Cell phones are safe indoors.

### Field Tactics in the Backcountry

- **Spread Out:** Maintain a distance of at least 50–100 feet between group members ("social distancing x 2") to prevent a single ground current strike from injuring the entire team.
- **Avoid High & Isolated Features:** Immediately descend from exposed ridges, summits, tall isolated trees, power lines, and ski lift towers.
- **Isolate Metal Gear:** Remove all metal equipment (ski poles, ice axes, tent poles, stoves, pots). Separate metal items so ground current does not fuse them together.
- **Crouch Low:** If trapped in the open, crouch on your sleeping pad or daypack with feet close together and hands over ears. **Do NOT lie flat on the ground**, as ground current travels horizontally through soil.
- **Move Quickly:** Running decreases the duration both feet contact the ground simultaneously, minimizing ground current shock risk.

### Emergency Care for Strike Victims

- **Safe to Touch:** Lightning victims do **not** retain an electrical charge and are immediately safe to handle.
- **Call 911:** Cell phone emergency calls automatically transmit GPS coordinates to search and rescue dispatchers.
- **CPR Protocol:** 85% of lightning fatalities stem from sudden cardiac arrest. Check the carotid artery for a pulse. Perform CPR continuously until EMTs arrive.

## 5 Ways Lightning Can Strike & Warning Signs

### Strike Mechanisms

1. **Direct Strike:** Lightning strikes a person directly. Least common, but most fatal as current passes through the cardiovascular and nervous systems.
2. **Side Flash:** Current strikes a nearby object (such as a tall tree) and jumps through the air to a person standing within 1–2 feet.
3. **Ground Current:** Current strikes a tree or ground point and spreads outward through the soil. **Causes the highest number of lightning deaths and injuries.**
4. **Conduction:** Current travels through metal fences, wires, plumbing, or long metal objects to a person.
5. **Streamers:** Positively charged ion streamers extend upward from the ground or a person to connect with descending negative step leaders.

!!! danger "Warning Signs of an Immediate Strike"

    If you experience any of the following, a lightning strike is imminent within seconds:

    - Hair standing on end or skin tingling
    - A metallic taste in your mouth or the smell of chlorine (ozone)
    - Buzzing, crackling, or vibrating sounds from metal gear
    
    **Action:** Run immediately to lower ground or drop into a low crouch on your pack!

## Atmospheric Optics & Sky Phenomena

### Why is the Sky Blue?

![Rayleigh Scattering Diagram](../../assets/images/11252021748.gif)
_Rayleigh Scattering Diagram._

Sunlight appears white, containing all wavelengths of the visible spectrum. As sunlight enters the upper atmosphere, short blue wavelengths scatter off oxygen molecules and atmospheric dust far more efficiently than longer red wavelengths, filling the sky with blue light. At sunrise and sunset, sunlight travels through a thicker atmospheric path at the horizon, scattering away blue light and leaving brilliant reds, oranges, and yellows.

### Circumzenithal Arcs ("Upside-Down Rainbows") & Moonbows

- **Smile Rainbows:** Circumzenithal arcs form when sunlight refracts through flat, horizontal ice crystals in high cirrus clouds, creating an inverted, upside-down rainbow.
- **Moonbows:** Rare nighttime rainbows produced when a bright full moon refracts light through falling rain.

### Northern Lights (Aurora Borealis & Australis)

![Aurora Borealis Solar Particle Diagram](../../assets/images/11252021806.jpg)
_Aurora Borealis Solar Particle Diagram._

Auroras occur when solar wind storms collide with Earth's magnetosphere 50 to 100 miles high. Energetic solar particles excite atmospheric gases: excited oxygen emits green and red light, while nitrogen emits violet and crimson hues.

### Crepuscular Rays, Sun Dogs & Sun Pillars

![Crepuscular Rays and Sun Pillars Diagram](../../assets/images/11252021808a.jpg)
_Crepuscular Rays and Sun Pillars Diagram._

- **Crepuscular Rays:** Alternating shafts of sunlight and shadow radiating through cloud gaps on the horizon.
- **Sun Dogs (Parhelia):** Bright mock suns appearing 22° to the left and right of the Sun when ice crystals in high cirrus clouds refract light.
- **Sun Pillars:** Vertical columns of light extending above or below the Sun near sunrise or sunset, caused by light reflecting off hexagonal ice plates floating horizontally in cold air.
