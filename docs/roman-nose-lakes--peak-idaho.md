# Roman Nose Lakes & Peak (Trail #160 & #165)

## Description

The views from the summit of Roman Nose Peak are among the finest in the American Selkirks. One of the
highlights of Roman Nose Lakes is that the trailhead sits immediately adjacent to Lake #1, featuring an ADA
wheelchair-accessible boardwalk around part of the shore and a well-maintained campground to the left.

The main trail to the upper lakes ascends through a young forest recovering from the historic 1967 Sundance
Fire, providing low canopy cover and expansive panoramic views. Lower Lake #1 rests in a half-bowl framed by
steep granite cliffs on the south and west. As the trail skirts the west wall, it ascends to a high point
(6,410') overlooking a seasonal cascading waterfall dropping off Lake #2.

From the high point, the trail drops into the upper cirque basin containing Lake #2 and Lake #3. Lake #3 is
encased in sheer granite walls with sunbathing and swimming ledges on its southwest shore.

## Route Options & Scrambles

### Option 1: Roman Nose Peak via Whisker Ridge

The scramble to Roman Nose Peak (7,260') via Whisker Ridge is 4.3 miles round-trip with 1,588' elevation
gain. From the trailhead at Lake #1, head cross-country up to the eastern ridge overlooking the lake. Once
on Whisker Ridge (named for its sparse, bleached white snags created by the 1967 fire), head west-southwest
along high subalpine meadows towards the peak.

The ridge offers spectacular boulder scrambles along cliff edges. The final ascent follows a historic
lookout trail to the summit, where a USFS fire lookout tower stood until it was destroyed by high winds
in 2007.

### Option 2: Roman Nose Lake #2 & Hidden Waterfall

From the main trail high point, follow the directional post down toward Lake #2. Step out onto granite slabs
to view the outlet stream, which roars into a cascading waterfall during spring runoff. The trail drops
nearly 300' to Lake #2, where a primitive shoreline path leads south to a hidden 50+ foot waterfall tucked
in the trees.

### Option 3: Lake #3 & Glacial Cirque Basin

Continue west along the main path past the Nature Trail junction to reach Lake #3. Follow the southeast
shoreline past dispersed campsites to the base of the cirque wall directly below Roman Nose Peak (7,260').

### Option 4: Interpretive Nature Loop Trail

On the return descent from Lake #3, follow the metal directional markers for the Nature Trail Loop. This
1-mile loop climbs slightly before dropping down through a subalpine fir forest via switchbacks, returning
to the lower trailhead.

## Directions

From Bonners Ferry, drive south through town and turn left onto Riverside Street just before the Kootenai
River bridge. Drive west for 5 miles to Forest Road #417 (FR 18). Turn left (south) for 2.5 miles, then turn
right onto FR #402 before the Lion's Club Picnic Area. Drive 1.1 miles past Snow Creek Falls, then continue
on FR #402 for 16.8 miles to FR #2667. Turn right and drive 0.5 miles to the trailhead.

## Hazards & Safety Tips

The main trail to the lakes and Nature Loop presents no major hazards. However, the Whisker Ridge scramble
requires sure-footed boulder hopping and exposure along cliff edges. Always watch your footing on loose
granite.

## History: The Sundance Fire (September 23, 1967)

The Selkirk Mountains still bear prominent scars from the catastrophic Sundance Fire. Started by a lightning
strike on August 23, 1967 near the Sundance Lookout Tower near Coolin, the fire initially burned 35 acres.
One week later, severe drought and 50+ mph winds exploded the smoldering blaze.

On September 1, 1967, the fire storm traveled **16 miles in just 9 hours**, consuming over 55,000 acres of
timber. The inferno generated winds strong enough to snap mature old-growth trees and heat intense enough to
split solid granite boulders. Over 2,000 firefighters combated the blaze until autumn rains brought control
in mid-September.
