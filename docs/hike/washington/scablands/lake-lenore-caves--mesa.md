# Lake Lenore Caves & Mesa

## Description

From the parking area along Lake Lenore, follow the signs and the paved trail up to the first cave. From
here, the remaining caves must be explored on foot. There are seven caves in total to explore.

---

## Route Options

### Option 1: High Mesa & Side Canyon Exploration

To extend your day, after leaving the paved trail, look for a route up through the basalt cliffs. Up on top,
hiking options extend in all directions across the high mesa. If you venture onto the high mesa, bring a map
or satellite images, as the scabland terrain is quite unique.

Keeping Lake Lenore in sight, a series of trails leads back down to Highway 17 and the parking area. Up on
top, you will find Lake Lenore Side Canyon and numerous ice-age flood potholes created over 12,000 years ago
during the Great Floods.

---

## Directions

From Spokane, drive west on Hwy 2 for about 2 hours to Hwy 155. Turn left (south) on 155 past Coulee City,
then turn left (southwest) on Hwy 17.

On your way to Lake Lenore, consider stopping at the **Dry Falls Visitors Center**—the cliffs below were
once the largest waterfall in the world. Next along Hwy 17 is **Sun Lakes-Dry Falls State Park**, offering
numerous recreational activities. Continue southwest on Hwy 17 past Alkali Lake to the Lake Lenore parking
area.

If you have kayaks, bring them along! Paddling Lake Lenore is delightful, featuring several islands near the
south end to explore.

## Cool Things Close By

## Hazards & Safety

!!! caution "Scabland Hiking Hazards & Snake Safety"
    Hiking in the Channeled Scablands involves specific hazards:

    - **Unusual Terrain & Navigation:** The maze-like mesa terrain can be confusing. Take topographic maps, offline GPS maps, or satellite images on your phone for reference.
    - **Rattlesnakes:** Rattlesnakes inhabit the scablands, often sunning themselves on rocks in the morning. Be cautious when stepping over rocks or logs. They generally do not strike unless provoked. Consider wearing snake-bite-proof gaiters for extra protection.
    - **Visual Navigation:** Periodically take photo references of distant mesas, coulees, and landmarks to maintain situational awareness.
