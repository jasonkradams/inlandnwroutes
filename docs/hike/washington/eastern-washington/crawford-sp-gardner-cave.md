# Crawford State Park & Gardner Cave

## Description

Gardner Cave is located northwest of Boundary Dam in northeastern Washington at Crawford State Park. The
cave system is 1,055 feet long, with 494 feet accessible to visitors on guided tours. Inside, the cavern
features stalactites, stalagmites, flowstones, rimstone pools, and a 2-foot-diameter column. Near the
entrance, visitors can look up at the ceiling to see tree roots penetrating from the surface.

A sturdy stairway descends 125 feet into the cave, illuminated with electric lighting for photography and
viewing. The cave maintains a constant temperature of 39°F year-round, so warm clothing is recommended.
Guided tours take visitors to the end of the public passage, where guides temporarily turn off the lights to
demonstrate absolute pitch darkness and silence.

Access to Gardner Cave is allowed strictly via guided tours provided by Washington State Parks to preserve
delicate cave formations. Please do not touch any natural features or stalactites; skin oils disrupt mineral
water deposition and permanently damage formations.

In 1899, Ed Gardner discovered the caves and was rumored to have used them to store bootleg alcohol. The
cave is open to the public during summer months only.

## Park Overview & Cave Features

Crawford State Park Heritage Site feels like it is at the end of the earth, and its star attraction, Gardner
Cave, feels like the center of the earth. Tucked away in the northeast corner of Washington near the
U.S.–Canada and Washington–Idaho borders, Crawford State Park is a 40-acre forested day-use park.

Gardner Cave is a 500-million-year-old cavern measuring approximately 2,072 feet in length and 295 feet in
depth. Hailed as a "natural wonder" by a 1911 federal land survey, it is one of Washington's longest caves
and offers easy access to one of the most intriguing underground landscapes in the western United States.

## Directions

Located 11 miles north of Metaline, Washington, in the extreme northeastern corner of the state in Pend
Oreille County.

**From Spokane:** Take Hwy 2 north to SR 211 and turn left. Continue to SR 20 and turn left again, heading
north. At Tiger, SR 20 turns left to Colville; continue heading north on SR 31. Follow signs through Ione,
Metaline, and Metaline Falls. From Metaline, follow highway signs to Crawford State Park.

## Cool Things Close By

## Park Features & Facilities

### Discover Pass Requirements

A Discover Pass is required for vehicle access to state parks for day use. For more information about the
Discover Pass and exemptions, visit the
[Discover Pass website](https://parks.state.wa.us/167/Discover-Pass-Fees).

### ADA Amenities & Facilities

- Restroom facilities are available.

Use the interactive [ADA Recreation Map](https://parks.state.wa.us/156/ADA-Recreation) to search for other
state parks with accessible amenities.

### Picnic & Day-Use Facilities

The park provides one picnic shelter without electricity, plus two sheltered and 11 unsheltered picnic
tables. Facilities are available on a first-come, first-served basis. Picnic tables are situated in pleasant
locations, including near the cave entrance and along the U.S.–Canada border.

## Gardner Cave Tours

Tours run Thursday through Monday at **10 a.m., 12 p.m., 2 p.m., and 4 p.m.** during the operating season.

## Additional Information

- Tour groups are limited to 25 people. On holidays and busy summer days, extra tours may be added. Large

- Sturdy walking shoes or hiking boots are recommended for touring the cave and surrounding trails.
