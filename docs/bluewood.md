## Description

**Ski Bluewood**, formerly known as "Bluewood Ski Area," is an
[alpine](https://kids.kiddle.co/Alpine_skiing) [ski area](https://kids.kiddle.co/Ski_resort) in the
northwestern [United States](https://kids.kiddle.co/United_States), in southeastern
[Washington](https://kids.kiddle.co/Washington_(state)). Located in [Columbia
County](https://kids.kiddle.co/Columbia_County,_Washington), at the northern end of the [Blue
Mountains](https://kids.kiddle.co/Blue_Mountains_(Pacific_Northwest)) in the Umatilla National Forest, the
[elevation](https://kids.kiddle.co/Elevation) at the base area is 4,545 feet (1,385 m) above sea level, with
a summit of 5,670 feet (1,728 m) for a vertical drop of 1,125 feet (343 m). The northward-facing slopes are
about four miles (6.5 km) north of the [Oregon](https://kids.kiddle.co/Oregon) border and fifty miles (80
km) west of [Idaho](https://kids.kiddle.co/Idaho), part of the headwaters of the [Touchet
River](https://kids.kiddle.co/Touchet_River), the main tributary of the [Walla Walla
River](https://kids.kiddle.co/Walla_Walla_River). The only surface access to the base area of the mountain
is through [Dayton](https://kids.kiddle.co/Dayton,_Washington), 22 miles (35 km) to the northwest and nearly
3,000 feet (900 m) below on North Touchet Road. Though the summit is only about twenty miles (30 km) due
east of [Walla Walla](https://kids.kiddle.co/Walla_Walla,_Washington) as the crow flies, Ski Bluewood is
about an hour's drive as Dayton is thirty miles (50 km) northeast on U.S. Route 12. In addition to Walla
Walla, Bluewood is the closest alpine ski area (in mileage) to the
[Tri-Cities](https://kids.kiddle.co/Tri-Cities,_Washington) to the west, and the closest
[chairlift](https://kids.kiddle.co/Chairlift)-served area to
[Lewiston](https://kids.kiddle.co/Lewiston,_Idaho)-[Clarkston](https://kids.kiddle.co/Clarkston,_Washington).

HistoryOriginally conceived in the 1960s and created by Skyline Basin Associates during the 1970s, the ski
area's original name (as a concept) was "Skyline Basin." After years of planning, fundraising, and approval
procedures, the ski area opened as "Bluewood Ski Area" in January 1980. The second season of 1980–81 was a
very poor snow year, with only three days of skiing, and the fledgling ski area filed for [Chapter 11
bankruptcy](https://kids.kiddle.co/Bankruptcy) protection in August 1981. Auctioned off two years later and
acquired by Rainier Bank, it was purchased for $550,000 in October 1983 by
[Portland](https://kids.kiddle.co/Portland,_Oregon) executive Stan Goodell, a former president of the ski
patrol at [Mount Hood](https://kids.kiddle.co/Mount_Hood_Skibowl). To distance itself from the past
financial difficulties, Goodell renamed it "Ski Bluewood" and relocated to Dayton to run the area himself.
An expansion in the summer of 1986 added parking and the Triple Nickel, a triple chairlift to serve the
instructional area with a vertical rise of 400 feet (120 m). It joined the existing lifts, the Skyline
Express triple chair and Easy Rider platter lift, which was relocated.
