# Bloom Peak

## Description

One of the highlight features of Bloom Peak is that you can drive to within 161 vertical feet of the summit. Bloom Peak sits on a long ridge that forms the Idaho-Montana border, located east of Devil's Elbow camp on the North Fork Coeur d'Alene River. The views in all directions are well worth the drive.

Long ridge hikes extend both north and south with unobstructed views. To the northeast, views of the Cabinet Mountains Wilderness are impressive. Bloom Peak can also be accessed from the upper reaches of Settlers Grove of Ancient Cedars Trail #162.

Just to the east is Ninetythree Mile Lake for water (the numerical lake/peak names indicate their distance down the border from Canada).

This ridge burned decades ago, and silver snags offer great photography foregrounds. Because vehicle access is available, motorized recreation (ORVs and motorcycles) also uses the area.

## Option #1: North State Line Ridge

Heading north on the State Line Ridge leads towards Lost Peak (6,250'), Eightyseven Mile Peak (5,617'), Porcupine Pass (5,617'), Botto Peak (6,305'), Twin Peaks, Eightymile Peak, and Idaho Peak (6,506').

## Option #2: South State Line Ridge

Heading south along the border ridge leads towards Black Peak (6,546').

## Directions

Drive east on I-90 to the Kingston exit and turn left (north) over the freeway. Drive up the Coeur d'Alene River past Prichard to just past the Avery Creek Picnic area. Less than 0.5 miles past Avery Creek, turn onto Lost Creek Road #442. Continue on Road #442 to Taylor Saddle, then turn right (east) onto F.R. #430 to Bloom Peak.

## Cool Things Close By

Little Guard Lookout, Shadow Falls, Fern Falls, Centennial Falls, Settlers Grove of Ancient Cedars, Revett Lake, and Upper & Lower Blossom Lakes.

## Hazards

Motorcycles and ORVs may be active on the road and ridgeline.

## R & P

The Enaville Snake Pit, and Kingston Hilltop Bar & Grill in Kingston.
