# Dry Falls & Sun Lakes State Park

## Description

If you have never been to the Dry Falls area, you will be amazed at what the landscape represents. The last outburst of the Glacial Lake Missoula Floods occurred roughly 15,000 years ago, following numerous previous flood cycles. As you gaze out over the terrain from the visitor center on Highway 17, imagine a wall of water over 1,000 feet tall rushing at 55 mph over the lip of the plunge pool below. The volume of water released from the Clark Fork region in northern Idaho and Montana was equivalent to Lakes Erie and Superior combined, discharging most of its massive volume in just a day and a half.

Dry Falls was once four times the size of Niagara Falls and stands as a testament to the largest catastrophic flooding events in Earth's history.

## Hiking Options

### Option #1: Monument Coulee Trail

Monument Coulee is located within Sun Lakes State Park off Highway 17 near Coulee City. Drive through Sun Lakes State Park to the Deep Lake–Dry Falls Lake Road, approximately 1.2 miles from Camp Delany. Hike up to Camp Delany and follow the Monument Coulee Trail through gigantic basalt columns. Some columns remain standing, others lean at severe angles, and many lie scattered on the ground.

### Option #2: Dry Falls Lake Kayaking & Cave Exploration

Paddlers can launch a kayak or canoe at Dry Falls Lake to navigate beneath the 400-foot basalt cliff face of the ancient falls. Above Dry Falls Lake, hikers can explore Dry Falls Cave.

### Option #3: Sun Lakes & Visitor Center Trails

Sun Lakes State Park and the Highway 17 Visitor Center offer extensive trail networks across the scablands landscape. Trail maps are available at both locations for planning custom day hikes.

## Directions

From Spokane, drive west on US-2 for approximately two hours to the WA-155 junction near Coulee City. Turn left onto WA-155, then turn left onto WA-17 South toward the park.

## Hazards

- **Rattlesnakes:** Rattlesnakes are common throughout the Washington Scablands. During cool mornings, they bask in sunny spots on rocks and beneath brush to warm up. Consider wearing impenetrable snake gaiters or leg wraps when hiking off-pavement in scabland terrain.
