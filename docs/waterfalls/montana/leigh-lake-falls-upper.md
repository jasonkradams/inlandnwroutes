# Leigh Lake Falls Upper

![Paddlers rest below a 600' cascade along Leigh Lake's shoreline](../../assets/images/11132021628.jpg)
_Paddlers rest below a 600' cascade along Leigh Lake's shoreline._

## Description

Leigh Lake is a gem in Montana. It's only 1.52 miles in, but the trail gains 1,104 vertical feet. In the
last six years, two rockslides from high above have necessitated a rebuild of the trail, completed in early
summer. As you leave the trailhead, please fill out the trail registration card. These cards show the USFS
the use of the trails and are eventually used to fund trail projects. They can also be used in case of
overdue hikers and climbers.

At about .3 miles, notice a K.N.F./C.M.W. boundary sign posted in a tree above you. For the first .75 miles,
the trail stays in the woods and climbs semi-steeply until it breaks out alongside Leigh Creek. From here
the views become exceptional, and the hiking is easier. The middle section of trail crosses boulders that
don't require any real scrambling. A few switchbacks lead you to Leigh Creek Falls (see the Leigh Lake
Falls Lower guide).

During the trail rebuild, the USFS opened access to the old, original campsite; those changes will be
added here after a visit to confirm them. Leigh Creek Falls is an excellent place to shed the pack, splash
cold water on your face, and enjoy the most beautiful waterfall in the C.M.W. From Leigh Creek Falls, the
trail climbs steeply for less than 200 vertical feet. Even though the trail is steep, it's easily doable;
six-year-olds in flip-flops have managed this section without difficulty. Above this section, you'll have
incredible views and sounds of Leigh Creek Falls below the trail.

The walk to the lake climbs a little before dropping down to the water. In the spring, glacier lilies cover
the area the trail winds through. As you near the lake, the outlet creek is filled with weathered logs that
have fallen from high above. A little farther, and you reach the rocks at the northeast end of the lake.

!!! warning "Camping Restriction"

    The USFS and Libby Ranger District prohibit camping on this side of the lake lower than 300' from the
    shoreline.

### Option #1

From the first contact with the lake at the rocks, follow a user-created trail on your right (north) side.
In a little over .2 miles is a rocky beach worth visiting. In early spring, a creek coming down from
Bockman Peak creates a snow cave to explore. Keep in mind, it's cold and wet inside.

### Option #2

On the trail to Leigh Lake, a newer path leads to the old campsite on the southwest side of the lake.
Beware: the USFS closed this campsite years ago because people were cutting trees for firewood and leaving
garbage behind. Please be respectful of the land and the people who come after you, and carry out all
trash — aluminum and glass do not burn. Besides, if you can carry it in full, you can certainly carry it
out empty.

## Directions

From Libby, drive south on Highway 2 toward Glacier National Park for about 8 miles to the Bear Creek Road
\#278. Turn right (west) for three miles to FR #867. Turn right (west) for about 5 miles to FR #4786. Drive
up FR #4786 for about 2 miles to the trailhead. Along this last stretch of road, there's a pullout on the
left to a very primitive campsite. If it's occupied, you can camp at the trailhead parking area.

## Cool Things Close By

Snowshoe Peak (8,738'), A Peak, Granite Lake, L. & U. Geiger Lakes with Lost Buck Pass and the Cabinet
Divide Trail with views of Wanless Lake. The Proposed Scotchman Peaks Wilderness, the Bull River and Lake,
and the Clark Fork River.

## Hazards

All waterfalls are a hazard due to their slippery nature. Always be extra careful near any waterfall.
Please keep a close eye on your children when climbing the vertical section above Leigh Creek Falls.

## Restaurants & Pubs

Henry's Restaurant and The Shed in Libby.
