# Grand Targhee Ski Resort

## Overview

- **Location:** Alta, Wyoming
- **Named Runs:** 95
- **Lifts:** 6
- **Distance from Spokane:** 539 miles

## Description

Since 1969, Grand Targhee has been the home of skiing on the west side of the Tetons. With consistent
snowfall, wide-open terrain, and breathtaking views, it has remained a staple of the area since its opening.
While we’ve seen a few changes over the years and have more on the way, the Targhee vibe is still alive and
well. Since the lifts started spinning, we’ve embraced being laid back and keeping it simple. We were built
for skiers and we’ve tried to keep it that way.

## Historical Timeline

### The Early Years (1966–1978)

- **1966:** Locals conceive of a ski area and form Grand Targhee Resort, Inc., an Idaho non-profit
  corporation.
- **1969:** Resort is built including Bannock and Shoshone lifts, Targhee Lodge, and a small day lodge.
- **December 26, 1969:** Grand Targhee’s first-ever opening day. Over 2,000 skiers partake. Lift tickets
  were $3.75 and season passes were $55.00.
- **1971:** The Sioux Lodge opens and a master plan for the resort is adopted by the U.S. Forest Service.
- **1973:** GTR is purchased by Bill Robinson of Big Valley Corp.
- **1974:** The Blackfoot lift opens.
- **1977:** The Teewinot Lodge opens.
- **1978:** Expansion of Day Lodge, Steakhouse opens, and Lodging Office & Admin services expand into a new
  building.

### The 1980s

- **1980–1998:** Various attempted land exchanges take place over these two decades.
- **1987:** The Bergmeyers purchase GTR.
- **1988:** The resort adds summer operations.
- **1988:** Kids Club is built.
- **1988:** The 1st Annual Bluegrass Festival is held.
- **1988:** 1st Cowboy Poetry Festival—a 3-day festival that ran until 1993.
- **1989:** The snowcat skiing operation begins.
- **1989:** The Pagoda Ticket Booth is built (retired 2015).

### The 1990s

- **March 25, 1990:** Fire destroys the Day Lodge & Retail buildings. By Friday, March 30, using tents as
  temporary facilities, the resort resumed full operations. Mortimer the switchboard is replaced with
  rotary phones. By November, all base facilities were rebuilt and open.
- **1991:** Spa facilities open, Targhee Institute is established, and Rockin’ the Tetons (a 2-day festival
  that ran until 1998) begins.
- **1995:** A new Master Plan is completed for the Resort Area and Skiing Mountain, capping allowable skiers
  at one time (SAOT) to 5,130.
- **1997:** GTR is purchased by Booth Creek Ski Holdings, Inc. Shoshone is replaced with a fixed-grip quad;
  Bannock is replaced with a high-speed detachable quad and renamed Dreamcatcher. Resort enters process to
  exchange Squirrel Meadows to USFS for the base area.
- **March 1997:** Revised 40-year USFS Special Use Permit is issued to Grand Targhee Resort.

### The 2000s & 2010s

- **2000:** GTR is purchased by George Gillett & family. The Magic Carpet is installed.
- **February 25, 2000:** Single-day snowfall record of 41" in 24 hours.
- **2001:** Sacajawea lift opens, expanding terrain by 500 acres.
- **2002:** Trick Town Terrain Park opens.
- **2004:** The GTR-Squirrel Meadows Land Trade is completed. North Pole Terrain Park opens. The Steakhouse
  is remodeled.
- **2005:** The 1st Annual Targhee Fest.
- **2014:** For summer operations, the resort purchases a "Mule" for trail building. Two new cross-country
  biking trails are added, along with two downhill trails, a new flow trail, and the skills park
  (Pinball).
- **2016:** The Blackfoot double is replaced with a fixed-grip quad Doppelmayr chair lift.
- **2017:** Mountain Master Plan and Base Area Plan are revised and submitted to the Forest Service and
  Teton County, Wyoming.

### The 2020s

- **2020:** Massive wind storm blows down thousands of trees in Middle Earth, Lot 5, and service road to
  Blackfoot.
- **2021:** New state-of-the-art mountain operations building is constructed, $6.4 million is put into
  employee housing, and a new parking lot is built, increasing parking by 30%.
- **2021:** Construction begins on the new Peaked lift, with opening set for the 2022/23 winter season.
