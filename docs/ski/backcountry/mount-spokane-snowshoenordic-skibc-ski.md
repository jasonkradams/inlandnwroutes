# Mount Spokane Snowshoe, Nordic Ski & BC Ski

![Mount Spokane Snowshoe, Nordic Ski & BC Ski](../../assets/images/11272021806p.jpg)
_Mount Spokane Snowshoe, Nordic Ski & BC Ski._

## Description

It is really kind of nice to have one of the southernmost Selkirk Mountains in your backyard. Trail 130
circumnavigates Mount Spokane (5,883') with a low point of 3,693 feet and a high point of 5,183 feet if you
don't bag the peak. We did this route counterclockwise on snowshoes, probably the hardest way to do it.
There is a road on the backside of the mountain that slopes down from near the summit five miles to Chair 4.
If you are on skis going clockwise, you could coast down most of it. Going counterclockwise on snowshoes, it
was painful to trudge up.

One advantage of going counterclockwise is that you have shelter at about mile eight at the CCC Cabin to
warm up and eat lunch before heading back down two miles to the trucks. Going clockwise, the cabin would be
too early in the hike to have any benefit.

There really is not a good way to get across the ski area to properly complete the loop. We shuttled on the
road versus walking it.

## Directions

!!! warning "Permits & Red Tape"

    Starting December 1, the park becomes a snow park. Before December 1 you need a Discover Pass; after
    December 1 you need a Sno-Park pass. You need one or the other depending on the time of year, not both.
    If you plan to use the groomed Nordic trails, you also need a Groomed Trail pass. You do not need either
    to go to the ski resort. Both passes can be purchased online for $40 each at the
    [Washington Sno-Park permit store](https://epermits.parks.wa.gov/Store/SNO/SnoChoice.aspx).

Take Highway 206, Mount Spokane Park Drive, about 30 miles to the snowmobile parking lot. From there, start
hiking up Trail 131 to Trail 130, or take the Summit Road up. It is a pretty straightforward hike around the
backside of the mountain. Progress is measured by signs at each stream crossing, simply labeling them by
number descending down to Chair 4.

## Option #1

Hike up to the Bald Knob Campground and then follow the fall line straight up, staying to the right out of
the trees, until you peak out and eat lunch in the Vista House.

## Option #2

Nordic or skate ski the 36 miles of trails in this amazing park. Park up at the Selkirk Lodge, which has many
tables and a pot belly stove. There are a couple more warming huts out on the trails. The trails range from
easy to difficult, so you can choose the hard way or the easy way from point A to B.
