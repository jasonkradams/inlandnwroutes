# Mount Baldy Ski Resort

## Overview

- **Location:** Oliver, B.C., Canada
- **Named Runs:** 36
- **Lifts:** 4
- **Amenities:** Cross-country skiing, snowshoeing

## Description

Baldy Mountain Resort was founded in 1968. Our humble beginnings lie in a community of passionate winter
enthusiasts who saw the potential for amazing outdoor experiences. These enthusiasts overcame many
challenges and put their hearts into the development of Baldy.

## Mountain History & Operations

### Early Beginnings

Prior to the development of Baldy Mountain, the Borderline Ski Club operated a family ski area on Hedlund’s
Ranch near Bridesville. The season was short at that elevation, and skiers were looking for more challenging
terrain. The club had their eyes on Baldy Mountain, and a few members ventured on snowmobiles to check out
the area.

In 1967, a group formed a limited company, secured financing, and began the process of moving operations to
Baldy. The Borderline Ski Club voted on September 24, 1968, to transfer its assets and liabilities to Mt.
Baldy Recreations Ltd.

Following this, the last four miles of the access road were constructed, the lodge was built, runs were
cleared, and the T-bar was moved. Baldy Mountain Resort opened for the 1968–69 season with the McKinney
T-bar and beginner rope tow operating, along with Sunday afternoon snowcat rides to the summit for $1.00 per
ride.

In the early days, grooming was done by soft-packing with a culvert towed by a Nodwell snowcat. Once, when
the snowcat broke down, local kids were recruited to ski-pack a run. In the mid-1980s, after original owners
aged and a transaction fell through, the Borderline Ski Club leased operations, running a bare-bones season
until full operations resumed.

### Recent Revitalization

The purchase of Baldy Mountain Resort by an investment group in 2016 revitalized the resort with fiscal
stability and long-term planning.

Baldy Mountain Resort is located on the south side of Mount Baldy on the border of the South Okanagan & West
Kootenays, just north of the Canada-US border. Its base elevation of 5,700 feet (1,726 meters) makes it the
third highest ski area in British Columbia. The back side of Baldy Mountain can be seen from Oliver and is a
35-minute drive via all-weather public roads from Oliver and Highway 3 from Osoyoos.

The resort is renowned for its light, dry powder. Baldy provides terrain-based learning and activities for
everyone: alpine skiing, snowboarding, cross-country skiing, and snowshoeing, along with youth and adult
programs.

The top of the Eagle chair provides unobstructed panoramic views of the valley below. The mountain day lodge
features a cafe and bar, both recently renovated to welcome resort guests.
