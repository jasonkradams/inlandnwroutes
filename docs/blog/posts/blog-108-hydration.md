Hi InlandNWRoutes.com readers,

When temperatures get over 70°, we all must take measures to protect ourselves and especially our hiking partners from dehydration. BUT, dehydration isn’t only linked to high temperatures — we can get dehydrated at any temperature or under any conditions.

<!-- more -->

## Pre-Hike Hydration Strategy

Hydration for a hike should start the day before your hike. Getting hydrated and staying hydrated are two different things.

- **Day Before:** Start drinking cool water the day before your hike to get your body properly saturated. Limit your caffeine intake to as little as possible — zero is best in hot situations.

- **Morning of the Hike:** Start drinking water as soon as you are awake, and don’t stop for the rest of the day.

- **Activity Level:** There has always been an argument about how much water we need to drink, but what you will be doing dictates your need to hydrate.

## Warnings: Energy Drinks & Thirst Signals

!!! warning "Avoid Popular Energy Drinks"
    I KNOW THIS WILL BE HARD TO HEAR, BUT PLEASE HEED MY WARNING! **DO NOT DRINK THE POPULAR ENERGY DRINKS.** They are loaded with caffeine and can cause heart issues in even the fittest of us.

For proper hydration, do not allow yourself to feel thirsty. If your mouth dries up or you sweat too much, you could be in danger:

1. At the first hint of a dry mouth, start drinking enough water to quench your thirst.

2. Keep drinking water continuously so as not to feel any degree of thirst.

## Carrying & Filtering Water

Always carry a water purifier like the **Sawyer Mini Water Filter**. It weighs about 5 ounces, can filter up to 100,000 liters of water if properly used and cleaned, sells for about $27, and can save you so much misery.

I carry one or two water bottles at all times. However, if there is a lot of water along your route:

- You only need to carry one bottle full of water at the start to keep your pack weight lighter.

- As you get low on your first bottle of water, stop and filter enough water for whatever is in front of you.

- Planning your trip includes knowing where water sources are located along your way.

## Checking Your Hydration Level

A method of telling how hydrated or dehydrated you are is very simple: when you have to pee, notice the color of your urine.

!!! tip "Urine Color Hydration Check"

    - **Clear:** Properly hydrated.

    - **Slightly Yellow:** Okay at that moment, but monitor closely as it may change for the worse within 30 minutes.

    - **Neon Yellow:** **YOU ARE IN TROUBLE. STOP AND HYDRATE THOROUGHLY IMMEDIATELY!** Our urine should look clear. If it is not, start drinking or filtering water as soon as possible.
