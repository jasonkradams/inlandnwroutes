If you hike long enough, you will come across a scenario that requires you to call for help. If you have
cell

coverage,

call 911 first, or activate a personal locator device or other emergency alert system.

<!-- more -->

By doing this, you will get rescue crews on their way. When you call 911, they automatically get your
coordinates. But please keep in mind… it may take hours for rescue personnel to reach you.

Make your victim comfortable, listen to every word they say, and do no harm!

## Emergency Communication & Monitoring Vitals

I carry a pad of paper and a pen in my 13 Essentials just for this reason and for recording vitals:

- **Monitor Vitals:** Check heartbeat, breathing, temperature, and any visible injuries.

- **Log Everything:** Write down the time checked along with the results.

- **Control Bleeding:** If your victim is bleeding, do what it takes to stop the bleeding.

!!! warning "Tourniquet Precautions"
A tourniquet is a great tool, but it **MUST BE ADMINISTERED CORRECTLY**. Get instructions from a licensed
professional. An improperly applied tourniquet can cause more harm than help and is rarely used in emergency
care. What you want to do is restrict blood flow — not cut it off completely.

## CPR, First Aid & Shock Treatment

It would be a very good idea to take a **CPR & FIRST AID CLASS**. This way, you have the knowledge to help a
victim, hopefully without doing any more harm.

!!! info "Treating for Shock (Mayo Clinic)"
Lay the person down and elevate the legs and feet slightly, unless you think this may cause pain or further
injury. Keep the person still and don't move the person unless necessary.

## What Constitutes a Real Emergency?

There is something I must spell out: **having blisters and other minor injuries does not constitute an
emergency.**

- Emergencies are life-threatening and require immediate attention.

- If you call for help and it isn’t life-threatening, you may be charged for the rescue.

- It is all of our responsibilities to have the items needed to treat minor injuries on our own.

## Essential Emergency Gear Checklist

Items you need to have in your pack include the following (and there may be more):

- **13 Essentials:** Restock as needed (see: [14 Essentials](../../14-essentials.md)).

- **First Aid Kit:** Restock as needed.

- **Gorilla Tape:** Wrap about 3 yards around each of your hiking poles for quick field repairs.

- **Airway Mask:** Needed if mouth-to-mouth resuscitation during CPR or other circumstances is required,
protecting both parties from potential contamination.

- **Prescription Medications:** **Do not** give anyone your prescription painkillers.

- **Knowledge:** This is the most important item you can take into the mountains. **DO NOT GO UNTIL YOU HAVE
THE KNOWLEDGE.**

## Wilderness Training & Preparation

As a member of the Spokane Mountaineers, I know they offer Wilderness First Aid Classes. The more you know,
the more likely you can help others — and maybe even yourself.
