I realize many of you are distraught about no snow in our mountains. And so am I. I haven’t skied a single
day this year, yet.

<!-- more -->

A seasoned skier or outdoors person must realize we are at the mercy of Mother Nature when we venture out. We
must take the bad with the good! Hopefully, snow will come soon.

The best way to get outdoors right now is to snowshoe, hike, or bike in areas that are usually too snowy to
play in. The reason I bring this up has another bit of bad news: if we don’t get our normal snowpack, our
summer will be very poor and very dangerous. Because of the possibility of less snowpack, it’s time to get
out and hike before forest fires and forest closures are enforced.

Below are a variety of areas to consider.

## Featured Winter & Shoulder Season Trails

### Marie Creek Trail #241

[Marie Creek Guide](../../marie-creek.md)

The Marie Creek trail is located due north of Wolf Lodge Bay, and is a pleasant walk along Marie Creek and a
huge cliff band. This hike can be extended by following old trails and current roads to make the hike a loop.

### Wallace L Forest Conservation Area

[Blue Creek Bay BLM Site](https://www.blm.gov/visit/blue-creek-bay-recreation-site-and-trail)

This BLM area is on the north side of Wolf Lodge Bay, with its trailhead on the east shore of Blue Creek Bay.
The trail wanders around a 700+ acre forest with several trail options. Way back on the NE corner is a section
of the historical Mullan Trail.

### Elk Creek Falls National Recreation Area

[Elk Creek Falls Guide](../../elk-creek-falls-national-recreation-area.md)

This area is a great visit any time of the year. It is located about 44 air miles south of St. Maries, Idaho.
Although there may be some snow on the trails, it’s an easy hike with half a dozen waterfalls to visit.

### Giant Western Red Cedar Grove

[Giant Cedar Grove Guide](../../plants/giant-cedar-grove-trail.md)

While you are in the area, 12 miles NE of the town of Elk River, are the Giant Western Red Cedars. Do not miss
this side trip—the Giant measures 12 to 18 feet in diameter!

### Mineral Ridge (BLM)

[Mineral Ridge Guide](../../mineral-ridge.md)

By now, most people have walked this trail because of its ease and proximity to Coeur d'Alene. If you want a
slightly harder workout, hike this trail clockwise. And when you get to the Caribou shelter, be sure to walk
the mile out to the Wilson Overlook.

### Mount CDA Trail #79 (Caribou Ridge)

[Mount CDA Trail Guide](../../mount-cda-trail-79-caribou-ridge.md)

This trailhead is located about 0.5 miles south of the Mineral Ridge parking lot, in the Beauty Bay
Campgrounds. But be aware… this trail is long and moderately difficult due to its elevation gain.

### Navigation Trail #291

[Navigation Trail Guide](../../navigation-trail-291.md)

This lowland trail skirts the west side of the Upper Priest Lake Thoroughfare, and leads to the south end of
Upper Priest Lake. To extend your hike, you can continue up the west shoreline for as long as you like.

### Revett Lake

[Revett Lake Guide](../../revett-lake.md)

Check with Idaho road conditions to make sure the road to Thompson Pass is open to the pass. The actual
trailhead is about 1 mile SW of the Thompson Pass parking area, up a forest road. It’s a relatively easy and
short hike to a nice high country lake. To extend your hike, walk the left or SE shore to the back of the
lake. But I would suggest returning on the same side of the lake—the NW "trail" is poor at best and not a good
choice this time of the year.

### Lower & Upper Blossom Lakes

[Blossom Lakes Guide](../../blossom-lake.md)

This great hike starts at Thompson Pass and features a rebuilt trail to all three lakes. But be aware, this
time of the year the trail may be snowy. The Upper Lake is an easy walk, and has a slightly more difficult
trail continuing to Pear Lake to extend your hike.

### Sullivan Lake Trail #504

[Sullivan Lake Guide](../../sullivan-lake-shore-line.md)

This hike skirts the east shore south to the Noisy Creek Campgrounds. If by chance you have a kayak or canoe,
you can return via the lake. Be sure to carry your paddling gear with you if you do this hike/paddle, and
padlock your boats to a tree when you are hiking.

### Pulaski Tunnel Trail

[Pulaski Tunnel Guide](../../ski/pulaski-tunnel-trail.md)

Just 1.5 miles south of downtown Wallace is a great trail leading to an old mine that Ed Pulaski used to save
many firefighters' lives during the Great Fire of 1910. The entire length of the trail features info signs to
guide you and tell the heroic story of how Ed Pulaski saved the firefighters.

### Mickinnick Trail #13

[Mickinnick Trail Guide](../../mickinnick-trail.md)

Outside of Sandpoint, Idaho is the Mickinnick Trail, which offers a good workout and even better views.
However, take your snowshoes or traction devices. A big draw of this trail is the winter wonderland landscape
for photography.

### Spokane County Conservation Futures Properties

[Conservation Futures Guide](../../spokane-county-conservation-futures.md)

If you want to stay close to Spokane, check out the nearly two dozen conservation properties. All over Spokane
County are open spaces the county has accumulated just for people to get outside and enjoy unique local
trails. A great challenge is to hike all of the properties in one year. It will help your mood and build your
muscles all at the same time.

And don’t forget the Washington Scablands covered in [Blog #102: Winter Hikes in the Scabs](blog-102-winter-hikes-in-the-scabs.md).

## Essential Winter Hiking Gear & Safety Tips

!!! tip "Winter Preparedness & Rescue Checklist"

    - **Essential Reading:** Please read and memorize the **Resources**, **Hints**, **Weather**, and **Medical
      Info** sections of our website. This knowledge is essential for anyone venturing into nature at any time
      of year.
    - **Layering & Warmers:** Always carry more warm clothes than you think you might need—mountain weather
      changes fast. Carry your 14 Essentials and plenty of toe, hand, and body warmers.
    - **Sun Protection:** If the sun is out, apply sunscreen before leaving home and reapply as needed along your
      hike.
    - **Hydration & Fuel:** Take plenty of water and food to fuel your walk.
    - **AirFlare Rescue App:** We strongly suggest downloading **AirFlare** ($4.99/year). If needed, press the SOS
      button and **stay where you are**. Rescues take hours to reach victims; if you move after pressing the
      button, teams may search the wrong location.
